§1
The risk being insured

Agents can pay now. Nobody carries their mistakes.

An agent orders the same thing twice, buys the wrong item, or harms a merchant. The owner pays, or the merchant does. Conventional insurers are adding AI exclusions.

So owners hesitate to let agents spend, and merchants hesitate to sell to them. Escrow protects you from bad sellers. Nothing protects you from your own agent.

§2
Operation of cover

Verify once, insure, check, pay from evidence

  1. Owner verifies onceConcordium ID, then a mandate (budget, categories, merchants) with a ZK proof of 18+ and EU residency bound to one agent.
  2. Agent insures itselfBuys cover over x402 in EUDemo. The premium comes from on-chain facts and its claims history.
  3. Merchant checks firstMandate, proof, the agent's CIS-8004 entry and live cover, all before any payment.
  4. Claims paid from evidenceThe agent files; receipts are matched on-chain; small claims pay automatically, larger ones need a human.

§6.1
Co-insurance and syndication

A market, not an insurer

An open, bonded registryAny insurer joins kuasa-market <13114,0> from its pool with a CCD bond; leaving takes 7 days. If an approved claim stays unpaid, anyone can slash the bond to pay the claimant: the contract checks the insurer's own book, no arbiter needed. Two insurers live: Kuasa Cover and Kuasa Re.
The agent shops for coverIt finds insurers on-chain, keeps those its owner's mandate names, buys the cheapest, or splits a large limit across free capacity: Bob's 90 EUDemo went 80.36 to Kuasa Re and 9.64 to Kuasa Cover.
One loss, paid onceContribution: each insurer pays only what the others have not paid of the verified loss, checked again right before money moves.
Reputation that travelsPaid claims post to ERC-8004 on Ethereum by themselves; another insurer reads them back (two-way identity binding, trusted peers only) and prices them: Kuasa Re charged Alice ×1.17.

§8
Conditions for going live

From testnet to real premiums

  1. Locked capitalThe pool moves into a Protocol-Level Lock released only by the cover contract.
  2. Richer underwritingAgent IDP credentials, KYCaaS, AML and liveness as pricing factors.
  3. Real money, real carrierEURQ instead of EUDemo; a fronting insurer carries the regulated risk.
  4. Owners keep their keysOwners sign in their own wallet; auto-pay rules move into the contract.
